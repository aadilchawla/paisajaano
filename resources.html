PAISAJAANO: Practical Money Skills for Rajasthan's Youth

Simple financial education for a stronger future

The Challenge

  • Many young people in rural Rajasthan lack basic money management skills
  • Families often struggle with saving money consistently
  • Limited understanding of basic banking services and government schemes
  • Vulnerability to predatory lending and debt traps
  • No practical financial education in most rural schools

Our Solution

  • Visual and activity-based learning (no reading required)
  • Focus on basic concepts: saving, spending wisely, avoiding debt
  • Hands-on practice with real-life money situations
  • Local language instruction by community members
  • Physical tools like savings boxes and picture-based budgeting charts
  • Connection to government schemes that provide financial support

Government Schemes

Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

  • Zero-balance bank accounts with no fees
  • Free accident insurance coverage
  • No minimum balance requirement
  • RuPay debit card with no annual fee

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana

  • Special savings account for girl children
  • High interest rate (currently 7.6%)
  • Tax benefits for parents
  • Can be opened with just ₹250

PM Kisan Samman Nidhi

  • ₹6,000 yearly direct support to farmers
  • Paid in three installments of ₹2,000
  • Direct bank transfer to avoid middlemen
  • Can form foundation of savings plan

Atal Pension Yojana

  • Pension scheme for informal sector workers
  • Contribution as low as ₹42 per month
  • Guaranteed pension after age 60
  • Government contributes ₹1,000 per year for 5 years

Implementation Timeline

Phase 1: Preparation (3 months)
  • Develop simple visual materials
  • Identify and recruit village facilitators
  • Build relationships with 25 initial villages
Phase 2: Initial Implementation (6 months)
  • Launch in 25 villages across 3 districts
  • Host first district-level Financial Literacy Day
  • Establish first 25 school programs
Phase 3: Expansion (12 months)
  • Scale to 100 villages and schools
  • Begin mobile financial literacy camps
  • Host first Annual Youth Financial Festival
Phase 4: Community Ownership (15 months)
  • Train village leaders to run programs independently
  • Establish village savings groups in all locations
  • Create sustainable support network

Community Seminar Structure

Setup (1 hour before)

  • Village square or school grounds preparation
  • 5 learning stations with colorful banners
  • Registration table with picture badges
  • Simple refreshments arrangement

Opening Ceremony (30 minutes)

  • Welcome by respected village elder
  • Short skit showing money problems and solutions
  • Introduction of local bank representative
  • Simple explanation of day's activities

Learning Stations (2 hours, rotating)

Government Schemes Corner

  • Visual displays of each scheme
  • Help completing applications
  • Success stories from beneficiaries

Savings Practice Station

  • Making personal savings containers
  • Setting achievable savings goals
  • Creating visual savings calendars

Family Budgeting Area

  • Using canvas budget cloth
  • Prioritizing needs vs. wants
  • Seasonal planning for agricultural families

Debt Awareness Circle

  • Comparing interest rates visually
  • Understanding debt dangers
  • Alternative options to high-interest loans

Youth Money Skills Zone

  • Age-appropriate money games
  • Simple earning opportunities
  • School savings club enrollment

Closing Assembly (30 minutes)

  • Recognition of participants
  • Distribution of savings boxes
  • Community commitment ceremony
  • Next steps and regular meeting schedule
